Three species, one rule. Colour survives as the light they stand in.

Today’s five skins are one set of art with five CSS filters on it — Golden Hour and Moonlit are the same plant under different lighting. That is a paint job. Real species give the same slot a reason to exist: the silhouette changes, the name is real, and the pick teaches something a beginner can use at a shelf. v2 cuts the taxonomy lesson. Arabica, Robusta and Liberica are all species, so the list is parallel with no hierarchy to explain. And there is only one gate in the whole feature: Plus opens the Studio, and all three plants and all four lights come with it.

Verdict

Two axes, not one

These don’t compete once you separate what they mean. A plant is which coffee you own — real species, permanent. Light is what it’s standing in — mood, seasonal, free. Three plants × four lights is twelve groves from seven items.

primary axis

Plant — the species itself

Three species with genuinely different silhouettes: Arabica’s slim tiered frame, Robusta’s broad bushy leaf, Liberica’s huge leathery paddles. This is the only version that reads as different at 64×64 in the Studio door without relying on hue, which also makes it the only version that works for colour-blind users and in both moods.

secondary axis

Light — the mood on it

Keep it, cut it from five to three, and stop calling them plants. Golden Hour, Moonlit, First Frost applied over whatever plant you own — the filters already work, they cost nothing, and as a modifier they’re honest. As a standalone “plant” they were pretending to be something they aren’t.

Why not five, and why not Typica

Your original instinct was right, minus one item. Arabica / Robusta / Liberica are peers — three species — so nothing needs explaining. Typica, Bourbon and Geisha are varieties inside Arabica, so listing them alongside it would have asked a beginner to hold a two-level taxonomy before picking a houseplant. Same reason Excelsa isn’t a fourth: it’s generally treated as a form of Liberica, so it belongs inside that entry, not beside it.

The lineup

The three species a drinker actually meets

Chosen for silhouette contrast first, shelf relevance second. All three come with Plus — the Studio door is the paywall, so there is no second gate inside it and the chooser shows no locks. The footer on each card is an art-pipeline note, not an entitlement. Art shown is the current single tree asset standing in as a layout placeholder — the whole point of the proposal is that these three would not look alike.

How they compose

Own a plant, choose its light

The Studio door goes from one row to two: Grove (which plant) and Light (how it’s lit). The second row is the existing filter code, reduced and renamed.

The chooser, restructured

Live preview on top, then role → name → latin binomial → a three-row strip in plain language: Home, Grows, Tastes like. No altitude figures, no species-vs-variety label. Three plants means full-width rows rather than a grid — thumbnail, name, and the one thing a beginner wants (what it tastes like), with no odd empty cell.

Every plant is owned once you’re past the paywall, so the chooser shows no locks at all — no free-vs-Plus split inside the Studio, and no third “earned” tier. Growth still runs all ten stages, seed to harvest; the species changes what the ten stages look like, never how many there are — so the stage logic and module milestones don’t move.

The commercial argument

A plant drop is a Plus beat — “Liberica arrives this month” is something you can put in a push notification, on the paywall, and in the store screenshots. Recolours are not: nobody resubscribes for a fourth hue. So the art spend buys a retention cadence, not just prettier trees. And if the cadence needs more fuel later, Excelsa, Racemosa and Stenophylla are a “wild coffees” set for users who already know the three basics — not base content a newcomer has to wade through.

Risks

Four ways this goes wrong

audienceDon’t let it become a botany lesson
The v1 draft of this proposal failed exactly here: five plants, a species-vs-variety hierarchy, and a third unlock tier. For a beginner the grove should be one decision with three answers. Every field on the card has to survive the question “would a newcomer care?” — which is why altitude figures and binomial ranks came out.
accuracyA wrong plant is worse than a recolour
Someone who knows coffee will notice if Liberica’s leaves aren’t oversized or Robusta isn’t bushy. The illustrator needs reference photos per species per stage, and the botanical tell has to be what the drawing exaggerates. Don’t ship a plant you can’t draw correctly — ship two.
legibilitySilhouettes must survive 64pxStudioDoor
The Studio door and the milestone thumbnails render tiny. Test all three at 64×64 in both moods before approving art; if two are indistinguishable there, one of them isn’t earning its slot.
naming“Skin” has to goTREE_SKINS
Internally and in copy. You plant a species; you don’t skin it. TREE_VARIETIES and GROVE_LIGHT keep the two axes from blurring back together six months from now.
