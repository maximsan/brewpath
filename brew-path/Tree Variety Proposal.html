Variety is the right axis. Colour should survive as the second one.

Today’s five skins are one set of art with five CSS filters on it — Golden Hour and Moonlit are the same plant under different lighting. That is a paint job, and in a teaching app a paint job is the weakest thing a cosmetic can be. Botanical varieties give the same slot a reason to exist: the silhouette changes, the name is real, and every pick teaches something. It costs art. Below is what I’d ship, what it actually costs, and how to keep colour without the two ideas fighting.

Verdict

Two axes, not one

These don’t compete once you separate what they mean. A variety is which plant you own — identity, permanent, educational. A treatment is what light it’s standing in — mood, seasonal, cheap. Five varieties × three treatments is fifteen groves from eight items.

primary axis

Variety — the plant itself

Five real botanicals with genuinely different silhouettes: Typica’s tiered horizontal branches, Geisha’s lanky sparse frame, Robusta’s broad bushy leaf, Liberica’s huge leathery paddles. This is the only version that reads as different at 64×64 in the Studio door without relying on hue, which also makes it the only version that works for colour-blind users and in both moods.

secondary axis

Treatment — the light on it

Keep it, cut it from five to three, and stop calling them plants. Golden Hour, Moonlit, First Frost applied over whatever variety you own — the filters already work, they cost nothing, and as a modifier they’re honest. As a standalone “plant” they were pretending to be something they aren’t.

Fix this before anything is written down

“Typica vs Arabica vs Robusta” isn’t a parallel list — Typica is an Arabica. Arabica and Robusta are species; Typica, Bourbon and Geisha are varieties within Arabica. In an app that teaches coffee, shipping that mix-up in a picker would be the most visible factual error in the product. The two-line card below solves it by design: species eyebrow on top, variety name underneath. The picker then teaches the taxonomy instead of muddling it.

How they compose

Own a plant, choose its light

The Studio door goes from one row to two: Grove (which plant) and Light (how it’s lit). The second row is the existing filter code, reduced and renamed.

The chooser, restructured

Same grid, richer card. Species eyebrow → variety name → latin binomial → three-row spec strip → one-line botanical tell. That is already the Dictionary entry format, so the picker can reuse the dictionary card component and each plant can deep-link to its real term. A cosmetic that opens into curriculum is worth far more than a cosmetic that opens into nothing.

Growth still runs all ten stages, seed to harvest. The variety changes what the ten stages look like, never how many there are — so Coffee Tree.html’s stage logic and the module milestones don’t move.

The cost

The honest art number, and how to not pay it all at once

This is the only real argument against varieties, so it deserves the real figure rather than a hopeful one.

ApproachFramesNotes
Today — filters10One plant, five hues. Free, and the reason the skins feel thin.
Naïve varieties50Five plants × ten stages, all bespoke. Correct and unaffordable.
Shared early stages38Stages 1–3 — bare soil, sprout, two-leaf seedling — are near-identical across varieties in life. Share them, diverge from stage 4 where leaf shape and branching first read. Cuts a fifth of the work with no perceived loss.
Staged rollout — recommended3 + 2×7Ship Typica (retag the art you already have) and Robusta (the most visibly different plant) at 17 frames. That’s the whole idea proven. Then one variety per season as live-ops.
The commercial argument

A variety drop is a recurring Plus beat — “Geisha arrives this month” is something you can put in a push notification, on the paywall, and in the store screenshots. Recolours are not: nobody resubscribes for a fourth hue. So the art spend buys retention cadence, not just prettier trees. That reframes it from a cosmetic cost into the cheapest content pipeline the app has.

Risks

Three ways this goes wrong

accuracyA wrong plant is worse than a recolour
A learner who knows coffee will notice if Geisha isn’t lanky or Liberica’s leaves aren’t oversized. The illustrator needs reference photos per variety per stage, and the botanical tell has to be the thing the drawing exaggerates. Don’t ship a variety you can’t draw correctly — ship four.
legibilitySilhouettes must survive 64pxStudioDoor
The Studio door and the milestone thumbnails render tiny. Test every variety at 64×64 in both moods before approving art; if two are indistinguishable there, one of them isn’t earning its slot.
scopeDon’t block v1 on it
The audit already has the Studio in the v1 cut. Retagging the current art as Typica plus the three-light row is a same-week change that makes the existing feature honest. The other varieties can land after launch without any refactor, because the data shape doesn’t change.
naming“Skin” has to goTREE_SKINS
Internally and in copy. You plant a variety; you don’t skin it. TREE_VARIETIES and GROVE_LIGHT keep the two axes from blurring back together six months from now.

Commission Robusta, stages 4–10Seven frames. The most different-looking plant, so it proves the axis with the least spend. Reuse stages 1–3.
Test at 64px, then decide on the remaining threeIf Robusta reads clearly against Typica in the door and the milestone list, commission Bourbon, Geisha and Liberica on a seasonal cadence. If it doesn’t, you’ve spent seven frames finding out instead of thirty-eight.
